# The story, plainly

**Spoilers for the whole game.** In the game this is told in pieces — by the crew, in the station log, on the ice, at the endings — and the journal's "What is happening to you" page assembles the pieces you have found. This is the assembled version.

## Where you are

Cape Ferrin weather station: four huts on a gravel strip between a ridge and a bay, on an island with no other people on it. The plane that brought you left an hour ago and cannot come back until there is light enough on the strip to land: the twentieth day. You are the cook, hired because nobody else would come.

There are three others. **Marit Solheim** runs the station; eight winters; keeps the log. **Dov Lindqvist** keeps the radio; three winters; weather to Base at eight and twenty, and a game of chess by Morse at twenty-two that he has never missed. **Teo Brandt** keeps the generator; twenty-four; first winter, like yours.

## What is happening to you

There is not enough food. The store holds fifteen days at four; the plane is twenty days away; the manifest Dov sent in September says fourteen crates were landed and signed for — twenty-one days. Four crates are somewhere else on the island. Nobody has said so, and the woman who signed for them does not miscount.

## Where the food went

Across the bay, on the sledge, the week before the plane. There is a hut over there — the original station, closed up in 1961 with its stove and bunks left in "against need" — and **Aksel Rue** is in it: the cook here for four winters until this one, sixty-seven, failed his medical in the spring on age and nothing else, and told he would not winter again. He came out on the August supply ship as a visitor and asked Marit for one more winter. She said yes, signed him off the ship on paper, and carried four crates across to him before the bay froze.

That is why your store is short: Marit gave Aksel a share of the station's food. Her plan for the gap was the 1958 emergency depot on the headland and the seals off the point — fetched once the ice held. The ice held late this year, the storm is coming, and she told nobody, because the one person who would have to report a fifth man is Dov, and Dov reports things.

## The other pieces

- **Dov's rule.** Standing orders: a person not on the wintering list is reported to Base on the next schedule, and Base decides. Base would send a flight.
- **1971.** The last time Base sent the plane in the dark — for a man with a fever — it went into the ridge on the approach and the pilot, Haugen, was killed. The sick man recovered on his own. The leader wrote: "We should have waited." Reporting Aksel means asking a stranger to try that again.
- **Dov's game.** The chess is with Sunniva, the operator at Base, and it is not chess. It is the only hour of his day that is his. It is also the other thing that can make him wait.
- **Teo's nights.** Teo cannot sleep in the dark, so he runs the generator all night for the lights. The fuel margin Marit planned for the storm is gone. On the first storm night the generator runs dry unless Marit knows in time — and if she knows, Teo will know who told her.

## The count

Fifteen days of food at four, and twenty to the plane. The depot on the headland (from the log, 1958) is worth three days at four once the swollen tins are left behind. A seal, with Aksel at a breathing hole on a still day, is two. Half rations double what is left, once Marit has agreed to it — and she will not agree until you know what she knows. Any two of those close the sum. Bringing Aksel in adds a mouth and his remaining crates, which cancel.

If the count is not closed, the store runs out on the fifteenth night, and an hour after supper Aksel walks in out of the dark with a sledge and whatever is left of his crates, because he had been counting too.

## The choices

Nothing forgets them. *X will remember that.*

- **Marit:** keep her secret, or tell her you won't.
- **Dov:** tell him there are five, and he will send it on the evening schedule unless you give him a reason to wait — Haugen in 1971, or Sunniva. Walking out of the radio room without giving him one is sending it.
- **Teo:** tell Marit about the generator nights (the fuel holds; Teo knows who told her) or cover for him (the station freezes on the storm night; he knows you knew, and would do the same for you).
- **Aksel:** bring him in to the table, or leave him his winter and carry across what he needs.
- **On the strip:** when he asks you to say four, say five or let him go.

## The endings

Each one's last page says what happened, what you did that made it happen, and what it cost whom.

- **Five on the plane.** The twentieth morning; everyone gets on. The page says whether the count closed and how, what Marit will answer for at the other end, what Dov did with the report, what Teo knows, and what Aksel had.
- **The dark flight.** Dov reported it. Base sent the plane in the dark on the twelfth day, in a two-hour window. It landed — the first dark flight in fifty years, and the last one killed the pilot. It took Aksel, and Marit, relieved on the spot. Three of you finished the winter. The page is honest that the flight went well and could not have.
- **Aksel stayed.** He was on the strip when the plane came and gone across the bay before the pilot counted heads, because you let him. The ship comes in August. Marit saw, and said nothing.
